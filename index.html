What is HATAI?

A collaborative ecosystem for socially focused technology development.

HATAI is envisioned as an alliance where technology developers, clinicians, researchers, institutions, care providers and communities can jointly identify problems, develop solutions, evaluate them responsibly and share knowledge.

01

Problem-led innovation

Start with needs emerging from people, hospitals, rehabilitation centres, care centres and communities—not technology for its own sake.

02

Evidence & responsible research

Support collaborative studies, ethical practice, transparent data governance, scientific review and reproducible knowledge.

03

Scale what works

Turn locally validated solutions into approaches that can be adapted, evaluated and scaled nationally and internationally.

Community-based research as a scale engine

HATAI encourages research that begins close to the people who experience the problem. Local partners contribute context, lived experience and practical validation.

  • Identify locally relevant challenges.
  • Co-design and test solutions in real settings.
  • Document evidence and implementation learning.
  • Adapt successful models for wider adoption.

Open collaboration & knowledge sharing

People can participate through open-source projects, technical contributions, field studies, community documentation, peer review and collaborative research.

  • Open-source technology and tools where appropriate.
  • Collaborative reviews and research publications.
  • Partnerships with national and international institutes and universities.
  • Shared learning across disciplines and regions.
Responsible innovation

Research and technology with governance at the centre.

HATAI’s collaboration model can be anchored in participant dignity, privacy, scientific integrity, responsible data handling and transparent research practice.

A

Ethics & participant protection

Projects involving people should follow the applicable institutional, ethics and regulatory review pathways before research begins.

B

Privacy & data governance

Collect only what is necessary, define roles and access, use coded data where practicable, and maintain appropriate security and retention controls.

C

Research integrity

Support transparent authorship, conflict disclosure, reproducible records, responsible publication and honest reporting of findings.
